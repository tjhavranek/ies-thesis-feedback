# Declaring your use of AI

Charles University requires a declaration of generative-AI use in every final thesis. This page
explains what protects you, and what does not.

**Verify before you rely on this.** The rules are recent and they change. The current text is at
`ai.cuni.cz`, which also hosts an official template and a generator. Where this page and the
university disagree, the university is right.

---

## The short version

Under the rules in force since June 2026, **failing to declare is the punishable act.** Declaring
is not. Undeclared use can lead to a negative outcome at the defence, to disciplinary proceedings,
or, if it comes to light after you graduate, to proceedings to revoke the degree.

So the instinct to keep the declaration vague is backwards. A short, confident, specific
declaration is stronger than a hedged one, and far stronger than none.

What actually gets students into trouble is not disclosure. It is a **mismatch between what they
declared and what they can demonstrate in the room.** An examiner who suspects more was outsourced
than was declared will ask you to explain a choice in your own methods section. That is the test,
and it is the reason the verification sentences below matter more than the tool list.

---

## What a defensible declaration does

**It uses the university's own template.** Then it cannot be attacked as non-compliant, whatever
an individual examiner thinks about AI. Start there, not from a blank page.

**It discloses by function, not by quantity.** You say what you used the tool *for*. You are not
required to produce a word count, a transcript, or a paragraph-by-paragraph audit, and offering
one invites a conversation about a standard nobody has set. "I used it for language correction of
my own text and for feedback on structure" is specific and complete. "I used it a little" is
neither.

**It pairs each use with the verification you performed.** This is the part that does the work.

**It reflects a conversation you already had with your supervisor.** The university's own
principles include consulting your supervisor about AI use. Doing that in advance converts a
unilateral disclosure into a plan someone senior already agreed to, which is a much better position
to be in at a defence.

---

## Wording

Adapt the university's template. The structure below is what a complete declaration covers. Fill
in only what is true.

> **Declaration on the use of generative AI**
>
> In preparing this thesis I used [tool and version] between [date] and [date]. I used it for:
> (a) feedback on the structure of the manuscript and on the presentation of tables and figures;
> (b) language correction of text I had written myself;
> (c) discussion of alternative identification strategies and of the choice of robustness checks.
>
> I formulated the research question, the hypotheses, the choice of data, the estimations, their
> interpretation and the conclusions myself. I ran all code and verified its output. I verified
> every cited source against the original. I discussed my use of AI tools with my supervisor in
> advance.
>
> After using the above tools I thoroughly reviewed and revised the content as necessary, and I
> take full responsibility for the final form of this thesis.

The second paragraph is the shield. It is what reconciles the declaration with the separate
statement that you wrote the thesis independently, and it is exactly what a sceptical examiner is
probing for. Do not include a sentence from it that is not true. If you did not verify every
citation in the original, go and do that, then write the sentence.

The final sentence follows the university's template; keep its wording.

## The record that makes this easy

The `AI PRE-REVIEW` line on your Seminar Card asks which model you ran and when. Six dated entries
across two years mean that when you write this declaration eighteen months from now, you are
assembling it from something you wrote at the time rather than reconstructing it from memory.

`not run` is a perfectly normal entry. The university's principles include the right to decline to
use AI at all, without disadvantage.

---

## What this toolkit will not do

The prompts in this repository refuse to draft a declaration that claims less use than the session
they are running in has just performed. They will not help you make a declaration vaguer.

That is not squeamishness. A public, course-recommended tool that helped students word their
disclosures downwards would, in the first case that arose, hand the student a defence of "the
course's tool told me to phrase it this way" and hand the examiner a much larger problem. Neither
of you would benefit.

---

## If your examiner is hostile to AI

Some parts of this faculty take a restrictive line, and at least one neighbouring institute has a
binding decree treating undeclared AI use as a plagiarism matter, with the thesis ungraded and the
case referred onwards.

This does not change the advice. It sharpens it. Against a hostile reader:

- Stand on the university's template, so compliance is not arguable.
- Keep the declaration short and functional. Do not editorialise about AI.
- Be able to explain, out loud, every methodological choice in your thesis. This is the only test
  that actually matters, and it is the one you prepare for at the JEM002-3 milestone.
- Have the supervisor conversation early and, if you can, in writing.

---

## What to do now

Talk to your supervisor about how you intend to use AI, before you use it much. Record each run on
your card as you go. Write the declaration from the card at the end, from the university's
template. Then make sure you can defend every choice in your methods section without notes.
