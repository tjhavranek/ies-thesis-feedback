# What's new

Changes to the prompt and the page, newest first. Colleagues who teach the seminars test the
tool on real theses and proposals, and most of what follows comes from what they found.

## 7 October 2026

- "Could decide the defence" is kept for weaknesses that seriously undermine the contribution,
  the design or the credibility of the results, and each point is marked on its own. When none
  is found, the answer says so and still says what would take the work further. Points that
  could decide the defence come first.
- For a meta-analysis, the tool judges the work by the best practice at
  [meta-analysis.cz](https://meta-analysis.cz) and names the methods it expects, even when the
  chatbot cannot open web pages. The page also points meta-analysis students to
  [meta-analysis.cz/ai](https://meta-analysis.cz/ai/) for questions about methods.
- Smaller fixes from testing on 24 simulated submissions: no repeated question about your
  seminar when you have already said, no needless hedging of facts the tool was given, a
  concrete next step for bachelor's theses, and the formal form of address in Czech and Slovak.

## 29 September 2026

- For a meta-analysis thesis, the tool now uses the supervisors' notes for meta-analysis theses.
  It reads them if you attach them, or opens them at
  [meta-analysis.cz/ai/thesis-notes.md](https://meta-analysis.cz/ai/thesis-notes.md) if your
  chatbot can open web pages, and it tells you whether it read them. It uses them for questions
  as well, and points you to [meta-analysis.cz/ai](https://meta-analysis.cz/ai/) for questions
  about methods.

## 19 September 2026

- The answer opens with one sentence on the main strength and the main weakness of what you
  sent, so the first thing you read is where you stand rather than a list of problems.
- The points that could decide your defence come first, ahead of the quick fixes.
- The page asks you to paste tables in as text when they are pictures in your file, because a
  chatbot cannot read the numbers out of a picture.

## 18 September 2026

- The tool aims higher. One defensible sentence about what your thesis adds is now the minimum
  rather than the goal, and it says what would make the contribution stronger in the time you
  have, up to what it would take to publish, when that is within reach.
- When your work is in good shape it says so, says what makes it good so you know what to keep,
  and then says what would take it from good to excellent in the contribution, the design and
  the execution.
- For a bachelor's thesis the same push comes as suggestions, the next step up rather than a
  fault. The bar for the degree is unchanged.

## 17 September 2026

- Claude Code and Codex now come as desktop apps, so the advanced route needs no terminal.
  Claude Code is the Code tab of the Claude app, and Codex is part of the ChatGPT app.
- The short paragraph at the top of the answer is written in plainer language.
- Anything the tool has not checked, such as a paper or a rule it names from its own knowledge,
  is flagged in plain words instead of the old "citing from memory".
- The instruction to answer in English was dropped. You get English unless you write in another
  language or ask for one.

## 16 September 2026

- First version of the current tool: one prompt for whatever you have, from an idea to a
  finished thesis, a reference page on what good looks like at each stage, a switch for
  bachelor's theses, and an optional character counter.
